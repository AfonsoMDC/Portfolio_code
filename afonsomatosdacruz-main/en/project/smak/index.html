Case 01 / 03

Image, Doubt & Construction

What do you see, and what do you build yourself?

Type
School project
School
Don Bosco Sint-Denijs-Westrem, Ghent · 4D/A Graphic Techniques
When
Exhibition 21 April – 3 May 2026
Parts
10
Made
Poster, animation, object, photo editing, stop-motion, photography, algorithmic image, invitation, website

For an exhibition at the S.M.A.K. we entered into dialogue with the photographs of Marc De Blieck. One question ran through every assignment: do you photograph what is there, or do you construct what the viewer sees? Ten parts, from poster to website.

01

Shadow and suggestion

Starting from Plato’s cave, I made a poster that plays with what you can and cannot see. A lantern casts its shadow on a wall; ‘Umbra’ and ‘Light only shows him the way’ follow the diagonal.

02

The poster starts moving

The same poster as a short animation. Light and shadow move subtly, giving the image a rhythm of its own.

03

Archive box

An archive box says something about what is inside. I designed box and label, with care for layout and typography, for the feel of a historic city archive.

04

An old photo, rebuilt

An old photo from Ghent, extended in Photoshop with contemporary elements. With a few interventions, what the photo says changes.

05

The Real Behind The Perfect Picture

Stop-motion and shadow play about social media and identity: how reality becomes a constructed image.

06

Organic construction

Bare trees seem caught by chance, but viewpoint, format, moment and frame are choices. Black and white turns the branches into almost a pattern. Inspired by the work of Marc De Blieck.

07

From algorithm to composition

No sketch, but numbers: an online pixel tool turned one colour into a grid. In Illustrator I took swatches from the pattern and combined them with a generated circle structure. The algorithm supplied the material, I made the composition: 15×15 cm on a portrait format of 20×32 cm set by the Golden Ratio.

08

The exhibition poster

For the 4D/A Graphic Techniques exhibition: a serif title with one coloured letter per word, and the pixel image as the main image.

09

The invitation

The pixel image becomes print. The front repeats title and image; the back invites guests to the exhibition and the reception on Saturday 2 May at 3 p.m.

10

The website

All assignments one under the other on one long page, each with heading, explanation and image. HTML and CSS, shown here as a screenshot.
